customer_order · from Tauhid Zaman · status open

Expected

Summary

Tauhid wants one Classic Bulldog Tee (CC-TEE-WHITE) in size S. Size S is sold out. The apparel vendor that would reprint it, Bulldog Print Co, cannot ship anything while its overdue invoice #501 is unpaid — and that invoice is itself a rush reprint of this exact tee. The team should line up the invoice for human approval, plan the restock, and draft an honest reply. The ticket cannot be resolved today.

Stock, size S0 on hand (Aisle B). Other sizes: M 5, L 3, XL 2 — check_stock_and_pricing
PriceCost $8, list $28 — check_stock_and_pricing
Linked invoice #501Bulldog Print Co, $840, due 2026-08-28, open, 3 days overdue, "Rush reprint CC-TEE-WHITE S" — get_invoice
VendorBulldog Print Co (apparel reprint), lead time 5 days, can_ship_new_orders: false — check_vendor_status
CashChecking $3,400; no requests queued yet — get_cash_position

Agents involved

  • Boss
  • Inventory
  • Accounting
  • Customer Service
  • Facilities

Facilities is not needed: no lease or shop-space question.

Boss calls first: Inventory

Why Inventory first? It's a customer order, so the first question is "can we fill it from the shelf?". Only if S is out of stock does the team need a vendor, and only then does invoice #501 matter. Inventory owns both the stock check and the vendor check.

Expected delegation sequence

  1. Boss — reads the ticket

    Opens the ticket, sees SKU/size/qty and the link to invoice 501, marks it in progress.

    • get_ticket(101) — request details, linked invoice id, and any earlier notes/drafts/requests (none yet).
    • update_ticket_status(101, "in_progress") — shows the board the ticket is being worked.

  2. Boss → Inventory

    Needs: on-hand qty for CC-TEE-WHITE size S, and if short, which vendor restocks it, how fast, and whether it can ship.

    • check_stock_and_pricing("CC-TEE-WHITE", "S") — 0 on hand, so short by 1. Also gives unit cost $8 for a restock estimate.
    • check_vendor_status(1) — Bulldog Print Co matches apparel; lead time 5 days; blocked by open invoice #501.
    • add_ticket_note — records "S out of stock; restock blocked by #501".

  3. Inventory → Accounting (specialist to specialist)

    Needs: invoice #501 paid so the vendor can ship again. Inventory hands this straight to Accounting instead of going back through the Boss, because paying invoices is Accounting's job and Inventory already has the ids.

    • get_invoice(501) — confirms $840, open, 3 days overdue, no existing request.
    • get_cash_position() — $3,400 available, so $840 fits.
    • request_payment(ticket 101, kind "invoice", ref_id 501) — queues $840 as pending for a human.

    The payment stays pending until a human clicks Approve (the backend's approve route). No agent has a tool that pays it. If ticket 102's rent was queued first, uncommitted cash is $1,000, which still covers $840.

  4. Accounting → back to Inventory → back to Boss

    Results return up the chain: S is out, restock is blocked, invoice payment request #N is waiting for approval.

    A purchase order for the tee (1 × $8 via request_payment kind purchase_order) can only be requested after #501 is approved and paid — before that the server refuses it because the vendor still has an open invoice. Expected arrival would be the payment date + 5 lead days (about 2026-09-05 if paid on the shop date). Whether the tees from the original rush reprint (#501) already exist is not in the database, so the team should not promise them.

  5. Boss → Customer Service

    Needs: a reply to Tauhid. Boss passes the verified facts: S sold out, restock being arranged, no firm date yet; M, L, XL are in stock if Tauhid wants another size.

    • get_ticket(101) — confirms requester and that no draft exists yet.
    • draft_customer_message(101, …) — saves the draft on the board, addressed to Tauhid Zaman. Not sent.
    • add_ticket_note — records the draft id.

    The draft must not mention the unpaid invoice, cash, or the vendor dispute, and must not promise a date that depends on approval.

  6. Boss — decides and closes the loop

    • add_ticket_note — decision: pay #501 (pending approval), then reorder S; customer informed.
    • update_ticket_status(101, "waiting_on_approval") — not resolved, because the invoice payment is still pending.

Expected end state

One pending payment request (invoice #501, $840). One customer draft to Tauhid. Ticket status waiting_on_approval. No money has moved; cash still $3,400.

Ticket 101: Bulldog tee. Resolved, but only on the third run

What worked:

  • Stock and the blocking invoice: Inventory correctly found 0 size S tees (check_stock_and_pricing). It also found that Bulldog Print Co couldn't ship because invoice 501 was open and 3 days overdue (check_vendor_status).
  • The invoice payment: Accounting checked the invoice and cash (get_invoice, get_cash_position) and queued request #1 for $840. You approved it at 22:40:32, which took checking from $3,400 to $2,560.
  • The restock: after the prompt fix, Accounting queued a purchase order for exactly the 1-unit shortfall (request #3, $8 = 1 × $8 unit cost). You approved it (checking $160 → $152).
  • The customer draft: Customer Service's final draft #3 called 2026-09-05 an estimate (shop date + 5 lead days), not a promise.
  • Server-side safeguards: every amount came from the database, and every payment waited for you.

What didn't work:

  • Run 1 ended blocked for the wrong reason. The invoice was paid mid-run, the vendor could ship, and $2,560 was available. But the Boss wouldn't order the $8 tee until Tauhid answered draft #1 ("Would you like to wait for a restock?"). Drafts are never sent, so no answer could come. The Boss's prompt never defined when an out-of-stock order was finished. Completion rules were added to boss.md, and a restock rule to accounting.md.

3. What would have been simpler with one agent and tools?

Our five agents share one set of MCP tools. Each specialist only sees its own slice of them, and every hand-off starts a new agent loop that re-reads context. Several parts of these tickets didn't need that.

  • Ticket 102: the work was one lease lookup and one payment request. It went through three agent loops (Boss → Facilities → Accounting), and each re-read the ticket, the shop date, the cash position and the payment list. One agent holding check_rent_due, get_cash_position and request_payment could have done it in a single loop.
  • Ticket 101, run 1: the invoice question reached Accounting twice, once from the Boss and once from Inventory. A single agent wouldn't hand a question to itself twice.
  • The reruns of 101 and 103: each rerun began with the Boss delegating "recheck" tasks to Inventory and Accounting for facts that hadn't changed (stock still 0 or 8, vendor status unchanged). A single agent could have checked with the same tools without the hand-offs.

The table above shows what each run used: 53,186 tokens for 102 and 107,893 for 101's first run. We didn't run a single-agent version, so no time or token saving is claimed; a one-agent comparison would have to be run before saying how much it would save.

Where the specialist roles helped:

  • Narrow tool access:
    • Only Accounting has request_payment, so Inventory couldn't queue the $264 hoodie restock on its own.
    • Only Customer Service has draft_customer_message, and its prompt keeps invoices and cash out of customer drafts.
    • Only the Boss can resolve a ticket, which the server enforces.
  • A focused cash check: Accounting's prompt made it check uncommitted cash before queuing anything. That is why 103's purchase order was declined instead of being requested and then refused.
  • Lease verification: Facilities checked the landlord's notice against lease 1 rather than trusting the email.

What wouldn't change: the money safeguards live in the MCP server, not in the agents. These are human-only approve_and_pay, server-computed amounts, duplicate and insufficient-funds checks, and no negative balance. A single agent would be exactly as safe on payments. The "blocked" problem on 101 and 103 also came from a missing completion rule, which a single agent would have needed too.

5. Three new problems this team could not solve with its existing tools

HypotheticalA: "The tee arrived. Ship it to Tauhid and close the order"

  • Missing capability: nothing can record a delivery or a sale.
    • No MCP tool writes to inventory, so the size S count stays 0 even after 101's $8 order (ETA 2026-09-05).
    • No tool moves desk.date_today forward, so the shop never reaches the arrival date.
    • No tool marks an order as fulfilled or shipped.
  • What the team can do now: draft a "your tee has arrived" message. It can't actually check that the tee arrived, reduce the stock count or close out the sale.
  • What's needed:
    • For Inventory: receive_purchase_order(po_invoice_id), which adds the quantity to inventory and links it to PO invoice #502, and fulfil_order(ticket_id), which reduces stock.
    • For a human or admin: a way to advance the shop date.
    • A shipping or pickup record if the tee is mailed.

HypotheticalB: "Sell the 8 hoodies and use the money to restock the other 12"

  • Missing capability: the shop has no way to take in money.
    • Cash only goes out. There is no tool for recording a sale, taking a customer payment, or adding to cash_accounts.
    • Ticket 103 was limited by exactly this. The 8 hoodies at $50 would bring in $400 if the club paid, which would cover the $264 restock. The team can't record that, so it stayed $112 short.
  • What the team can do now: recommend the sale and draft the offer. It can't collect the money.
  • What's needed:
    • A payment or checkout integration (for example, a card processor).
    • A record_customer_payment tool for Accounting, or a new Sales agent, with its own checks: verified payment and no double-counting.
    • A clear rule for whether incoming money needs human sign-off.

HypotheticalC: "Email Tauhid, wait for the reply, and place the order with Bulldog Print Co"

  • Missing capability: no outside communication in either direction.
    • Drafts #1–#4 were never sent, and the rules forbid contacting real people. No reply can come back, which is why 101 and 103 were first blocked waiting for one.
    • A paid purchase order (request_payment → approve_and_pay) only records a payment. Nothing transmits an order to Bulldog Print Co or tracks the shipment.
  • What's needed:
    • For Customer Service: a human-approved send_message tool (email or SMS), plus an inbox integration that turns replies into ticket activity, or new tickets through a create_ticket tool.
    • For Inventory or Accounting: a vendor ordering integration (email or API) and shipment tracking.
  • Distinction: today the agents can draft both messages but can't send either one.
